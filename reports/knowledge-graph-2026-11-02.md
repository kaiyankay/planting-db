## 2. Attributes (Y): what the claims link to

195 of 353 concept nodes are in use. Per plant, 19–24 of the 27 schema fields have at least one source; the concept categories with most links are Environmental Conditions (exposure, soil, water, three hardiness scales) and Morphology & Growth (KPU height / spread classes spanned by every numeric claim, form, habit, texture, colour). Height bands (Robinson, KG-R2) and vertical layers (KG-R5) are derived from those numbers: 105 height-band edges and 15 layer edges — canopy and understorey for the three trees, shrub layer for the four shrubs, ground layer for the two perennials, the fern and the two low shrubs (kinnikinnick and salal reach both).

## 3. Design knowledge (Z)

122 plant → design-concept edges, none *documented* (no source states suitability for a Robinson / Rainer & West concept directly): 33 **derived** — a sourced KPU landscape use names the concept (KG-R1), e.g. *Cornus sericea* → Screen and Barrier (RHS and MOBOT hedge / screening uses), *Polystichum munitum* → Ground cover, Filler, Accent, Grouping (GPP, RHS, OSU uses), *Cercidiphyllum* → Canopy and Ceiling (MOBOT shade tree); 42 **candidate** — every required trait met by the plant's own claims but no source names the use (KG-R4), e.g. *Cercidiphyllum* → Screen (above-eye height from six sources, dense habit from MOBOT; evergreen preferred, unmet) and *Gaillardia* → Matrix; 47 **unknown / contradicted** — kept and shown: a tree is not a ground cover, a perennial is not a ceiling, and *Vaccinium ovatum* ↔ Screen is still contradicted by its single "Upright" habit claim although GPP and the survey list it for hedges. Concepts no plant reaches: Drift, Scatter (composition patterns are planting-plan knowledge the claim store does not carry); Scrim is reached only as a candidate for katsura. 22 literature relations remain to confirm at page level.

## 5. Example paths through the graph (all real; ids are claim ids)

1. **Plant → shared trait → plant, with verified local evidence on both sides.** *Cercidiphyllum japonicum* —(`cercidiphyllum-japonicum:local_mature_size:cov01`, City of Vancouver, verified: median height class 7.6 m, 10th–90th 4.6–13.7 m)→ height class **10 – 15 m** ←(`cornus-nuttallii:local_mature_size:cov01`, verified: median class 10.7 m, 7.6–16.8 m)— *Cornus nuttallii*. Both also reach the Robinson band *Tree planting* and the *Canopy* layer by KG-R2/R5, and the two are directly co-listed by respondent r07. What the path does *not* say: that they should be planted together.
2. **Plant → design concept → requirement → other plants, with the status of each.** *Screen* (Robinson ch. 3/4) requires above-eye height **and** a dense habit, prefers evergreen. *Cornus sericea*: derived (RHS 'hedge' / MOBOT 'screening' uses) and supported by traits (height 1.5–2.5 m RHS, 1.8–2.7 m MOBOT; habit Dense MOBOT; evergreen unmet). *Cercidiphyllum*: candidate (no source names a screen use; traits met). *Vaccinium ovatum*: derived from GPP and the survey ("evergreen plant for a hedge") but contradicted by its one habit claim. *Polystichum munitum*: contradicted (never above eye level). The answer to "evergreen screen" is therefore huckleberry *with a stated contradiction*, not a list.
3. **Plant → practitioner note → derived use → other plant through the same use.** Respondent r08 (MLA student, studio and garden experience, mixed basis): yarrow "spreads vigorously under certain conditions, so use cautiously; large swathes can be beautiful" (`achillea-millefolium:behaviour_with_others:sv02`, warning, L1) → derived `landscape_use` *Group or mass planting* (`…:landscape_use:sv03`) → the design concept *Grouping* (KG-R1), which *Acer palmatum* (MOBOT "groups"), *Cornus nuttallii*, *Cornus sericea*, *Arctostaphylos* and *Polystichum* (GPP "massed as a tall groundcover") also reach — five plants, five sources, one shared design concept, each with its own evidence trace.
4. **Plant → habitat → plant, by rule, then contradicted by a placeholder.** *Gaultheria shallon* —(E-Flora habitat: forest, bog, coastal, CWH, 0–1,700 m)→ REL-R1 —(E-Flora: open forest, coastal, CWH, 2–1,650 m)→ *Vaccinium ovatum*: derived co-occurrence (`rel:vaccinium-ovatum:gaultheria-shallon:02`), plus the survey co-listing (r03) and the model placeholder "salal competes with huckleberry" (candidate, `verify_against` practitioner / Pojar). Three relations of three statuses on one pair; the brain lists all three.
5. **Free-text ask → recognised concepts → gated plants → attributed claims.** "evergreen native ground cover for a dry sunny slope in Vancouver" (`tools/kg_query.py --ask`) recognises Sun, dry, Evergreen, Ground cover, Metro Vancouver, Native; traverses design edges (*Ground cover* derived and supported for kinnikinnick, sword fern, salal, yarrow) and trait edges; returns kinnikinnick first (E-Flora native, exposed rocky sites, OSU evergreen ground cover, USDA drought tolerance high, RHS full sun), then sword fern, salal, yarrow, huckleberry — each claim with source, status, conditions and place, L0 survey reasons withheld (R2). It does not resolve the dry-site contradiction between RHS (average) and MOBOT / USDA (dry) for kinnikinnick; it shows both.

## 6. Evidence and conflicts behind the links

Every claim edge resolves to a claim with `source_id`, `status`, `context`, `place` and, where stated, `conditions` and `cultivar` (Test E: 0 broken links, 0 live edges from model or retired claims). The diagnosis records 67 disagreements (same plant and facet, different sources, no stated condition, disjoint values — mostly height / spread between UK, Missouri, Oregon, US-wide and BC sources, and USDA's shade / leaf-retention ratings against horticultural sources), 1 condition-dependent variation (salal height, OSU: ≤ 0.6 m in sun on poor soil vs 1.2–3 m in shade on good soil), 5 use-vs-trait contradictions, 341 plant–concept links resting on one source, 6 gate facets with no claim. None was resolved; survey c3 says designers want to see the disagreement.

## 7. Extensibility (Test F)

`tools/scale_test.py` built roots of 10 real + 1 / 10 / 50 / 100 synthetic plants through the unchanged pipeline (1.8 s → 32 s; `kg.json` 3.5 → 21 MB); every synthetic plant received taxonomy, growth form, bands, layers, design links and pairs; the interface reads `kg.json` only (`?db=` opens a test root). The eleventh real plant needs: `plants/<id>.json` with an entity block, its names in `synonyms.json`, then `cov_trees_to_claims.py --merge` (if a tree), `inat_fetch.py` + `inat_to_claims.py --merge`, `survey_to_claims.py` (re-run), `merge_t5.py` on its extraction, `derive_relations.py`, `pipeline.py`, `kg_tests.py`.

## 8. What the graph still cannot say

- Which plants *combine well* — the only direct evidence is co-listing; "how it behaves alongside other plants" is what six respondents could not find and what the database still has least of. A practitioner round that asks for combinations with location and site conditions would fill it.
- Composition patterns (drift, scatter, matrix as a plan) — no claim type carries plan-level knowledge.
- Anything at the Vancouver scale for the non-tree plants beyond presence counts.
- Verified literature values: all 534 unverified claims await review, and every derived or candidate link inherits that status and says so.
