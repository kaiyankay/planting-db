## 1. What was built

**A projection, not a new graph.** `kg.json` (490 nodes · 2,114 edges, every claim edge, rule and gap) stays the knowledge layer. `build_xyz.py` writes a reduced view over it — `xyz.json` — in which every visible node and line names the kg ids it stands for and carries the claims and sources behind it, so the interface shows the evidence trace without loading kg.json and without knowing any plant.

**Three entrances** (knowledge domains, drawn as three soft clusters, not axes):

| | nodes | lines | basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| X · Plant Classification | growth forms that have plants: Tree (3) · Shrub (4) · Herbaceous perennial (2) · Fern (1); Graminoid and Vine hidden; genus (9), family (6), cultivar (8) on demand | `is_growth_form` (M29, derived; *supported* / *to confirm*), `in_genus` · `in_family` · `cultivar_of` | classification, taxonomy and cultivar edges |
| Y · Plant Knowledge | Environment · Growth & Form · Seasonal & Ecological Performance · Maintenance & Risk (navigation only) → 104 concepts the claims reach | `grows_in` (108) · `has_trait` (253): one line per plant × concept, claims aggregated; height bands by KG-R2 (derived) | claim, height-band and entity edges; facet → (group, policy) table |
| Z · Spatial Functions | Ceiling / Canopy (`spatial:ceiling` + `layer:canopy`) · Ground Cover (`role:ground_cover` + `layer:ground`) · Screen · Scrim · Barrier | `requires_trait` Z → Y / X (18 lines, one per alternative concept in use) · `suited_for` plant → Z (21: derived 11, candidate 10); 29 unknown pairs listed as gaps on the Z nodes, never drawn | ontology `requires_trait`, design edges (KG-R1, KG-R4) |

Numeric classes, hardiness and BEC zones, months, toxicity and KPU uses stay plant attributes and line evidence (brief §2: no sphere per number). Roles, compositions and layers outside the five functions stay underlying (brief §8: no Z expansion). Navigation lines (entrance → node, group → concept) are typed apart from knowledge (brief §6).

**Status and verification are separate on every line**: documented · derived · candidate · unknown, and verified (only when a live supporting claim is verified, counts shown) · unverified · entity · literature. A documented use contradicted by a recorded trait keeps its line with a *conflict* tag (Cornus sericea and Vaccinium ovatum → Screen / Barrier). Shared concepts connect plants only indirectly; direct lines come from `relations.json` only, typed, one per pair and type with every record (14 co-listed, 1 companion, 2 layered-under and 1 competes as model-placeholder candidates on demand).

**Interface** (3d-force-graph 1.80.1 + three 0.186, both MIT, already vendored; studied: expandable-nodes example for incremental `graphData` with kept positions, graphify-viz for progressive reveal and dimming, ml-knowledge-graph for the neighbourhood / path / info-panel split): warm off-white ground, matte spheres with soft halos, fine translucent lines (solid documented, violet derived, dashed amber candidate, dotted navigation), sprite labels, orbit camera with smooth transitions aimed beside the card. Click a sphere → its direct connections by kind (click again → fold); click a line → evidence trace; search (`/`), Back (Esc, 80 steps), Reset, Fit, rotate, zoom; focus set lit, the rest dimmed; three demonstration paths run step by step.

## 3. Three real exploration paths (found by `build_xyz.py`, not written)

**X — From Classification:** Tree → *Cercidiphyllum japonicum* → Tree planting (height band) → Ceiling / Canopy
1. `is_growth_form` *Cercidiphyllum* → Tree — derived (M29), supported: OSU form, GPP / MOBOT / RHS landscape-use, City of Vancouver inventory (`cercidiphyllum-japonicum:form:osu01`, `…:landscape_use:gpp01`, `…:mobot01`, `…:rhs01`, `…:vancouver_context:cov01`).
2. `has_trait` *Cercidiphyllum* → Tree planting — derived (KG-R2), **verified**: four City of Vancouver `local_mature_size` claims (cov01, cov02, cov04, cov05) plus GPP, MOBOT and OSU heights.
3. `requires_trait` Ceiling / Canopy → Tree planting — documented, literature: Robinson (2016) ch. 3 'Tree Planting'.
4. `suited_for` *Cercidiphyllum* → Ceiling / Canopy — derived (KG-R1), unverified: MOBOT use *Shade tree* (`cercidiphyllum-japonicum:landscape_use:mobot01`); requirement check: Tree met (classification), Tree planting met (7 claims).

**Z — From Spatial Function:** Ground Cover → Ground-level planting (height band) → *Gaultheria shallon* → Shrub
1. `requires_trait` Ground Cover → Ground-level planting — documented, literature: Rainer & West (2015) ground-cover layer; Robinson (2016) ch. 3.
2. `has_trait` *Gaultheria* → Ground-level planting — derived (KG-R2), unverified: E-Flora and OSU heights (`gaultheria-shallon:mature_height:eflora01`, `…:osu01`, the latter *under 2 ft in full sun on poor soil*).
3. `suited_for` *Gaultheria* → Ground Cover — derived (KG-R1), **verified**: survey respondent 03 lists salal as evergreen ground cover (`gaultheria-shallon:landscape_use:01`).
4. `is_growth_form` *Gaultheria* → Shrub — derived (M29), supported: OSU habit, E-Flora height.

**Y — From Plant Knowledge:** Seasonal & Ecological Performance → Evergreen → *Gaultheria shallon* ⟷ *Polystichum munitum* → Screen
1. navigation group → Evergreen.
2. `has_trait` *Gaultheria* → Evergreen — documented, **verified**: survey r03, OSU, RHS, USDA (4 claims).
3. `has_trait` *Polystichum* → Evergreen — documented, **verified**: E-Flora, GPP, OSU, RHS, survey r10 (5 claims) — the two plants meet at the concept, an indirect connection only.
4. `suited_for` *Gaultheria* → Screen — derived (KG-R1), unverified: USDA *sheared hedge* (`gaultheria-shallon:landscape_use:usdaplants01`); requirement check: above eye level met (OSU, USDA), Dense met, Evergreen met.
5. `requires_trait` Screen → Evergreen — documented (preferred), literature: Robinson (2016) ch. 4, *to confirm*.

Sources behind the three paths: City of Vancouver Public Trees (open data), E-Flora BC, Great Plant Picks, Missouri Botanical Garden Plant Finder, Oregon State University Landscape Plants, RHS, USDA PLANTS, survey respondents 03 and 10; literature Robinson (2016), Rainer & West (2015).
